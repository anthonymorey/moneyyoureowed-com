Is Unclaimed-Money Recovery Legit?

Yes. Every U.S. state and territory runs its own free, official unclaimed-property program, and the two portals that search across most of them (MissingMoney.com and Unclaimed.org) cost nothing to use. The part that actually causes trouble isn’t the databases. It’s what happens after you find something: paid “finder” services are legal in most states but never required, and anyone who calls or texts asking you to pay an upfront fee to “release” money in your name is running a scam, not a finder service.

Last fact-checked: July 20, 2026

The short answer

Unclaimed-money recovery is a real, government-run system, not an urban legend. When a bank, insurer, or employer loses contact with you for a set period (the “dormancy period”), state law requires them to hand your money over to the state treasury, where it sits free for you to claim, with no expiration in most states, until you or your heirs come looking. MissingMoney.com and Unclaimed.org are the two official, NAUPA-run search tools, and searching them costs nothing. Paid finder firms exist and are legal, but they’re optional. A real one only gets paid a percentage after they recover something for you, never up front. Sources: NAUPA: what is unclaimed property; NAUPA: is it really free to search

How money becomes “unclaimed” in the first place

This isn’t money the government took from you. It’s money that was already legally yours (a forgotten security deposit, an old bank account, an uncashed paycheck, an insurance payout) that the company holding it lost contact with you about.

By law, once an account or property sits with no activity or contact for a set “dormancy period” (generally one year or longer, depending on the state and the type of property), the company holding it can’t just keep it. It has to report and hand it over to the state, which then holds it as custodian, not the owner, until you or your heirs come forward to claim it. Source: NAUPA: what is unclaimed property

The state never gets to keep it

This is the detail that makes the whole system legitimate rather than suspicious: the state is legally a custodian, not a new owner. It's required to keep records and pay out claims indefinitely in most states, and there's no point at which your unclaimed money becomes the state's money to spend.

The two official free portals

MissingMoney.com is the official, free, multi-state unclaimed-property search endorsed by NAUPA (the National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators) and run in partnership with participating state treasuries. It searches most states’ databases at once, for free, updated daily. Source: MissingMoney.com

Unclaimed.org is NAUPA’s own official directory. It's the right place to start, and it routes you to any individual state program that MissingMoney doesn’t fully cover. Source: Unclaimed.org

Heads up: MissingMoney.com may not load for automated tools

If a script or browser extension tells you the site is unreachable, that's usually a bot-protection quirk, not the site being fake or down. It loads fine in a normal browser. When in doubt, type the address in yourself rather than clicking a link from an unexpected text or email.

Legit paid finders vs. scam impersonators

Once unclaimed property is state-held, most states allow third-party “finder” or “locator” businesses to charge a fee for tracking it down and filing the paperwork on your behalf. NAUPA’s own guidance describes the fee as “usually a percentage of the total find,” and recommends contacting your state’s unclaimed-property office directly before signing anything. Source: NAUPA: is it really free to search

Exactly what a finder can charge varies by state, and not every state caps it. California is one of the more restrictive examples: under Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1582(a)(2)(C), a valid finder agreement signed after the property has already been paid or delivered by the Controller can't charge more than 10% of the recovered property's value, and has to be in writing and signed by the owner. That's a California-specific rule, not a national one. Check your own state's unclaimed-property office for its actual cap, if it has one, before you sign anything. Source: Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1582

The one rule that separates a legit finder from a scam

A real finder gets paid after the money is recovered, out of the money recovered. The FTC's plain warning: if a caller or text asks for personal information, or an upfront “processing” fee to release funds, that’s a scam. No government agency calls or texts asking you to pay to search for or release unclaimed funds. Watch for false urgency too, since state programs don’t expire on a countdown: “time is running out” or a claim period “extended just for you” are both scam scripts. Source: FTC consumer alert, March 2026

How to actually search, in under 10 minutes

Do this yourself, for free, before considering anyone who offers to do it for you.

  • Start at unclaimed.org/search or missingmoney.com, both free and official.
  • Search every name variation: maiden names, nicknames, and any name changes.
  • Search every state you've ever lived in, worked in, or held an account in, not just your current state.
  • Search for deceased relatives too; unclaimed property doesn't disappear when someone dies, and heirs can claim it.
  • Then check the federal-specific databases (pensions, failed banks, VA insurance, and more) that state unclaimed-property searches don't cover. See our companion guide on the databases that may hold money in your name.
  • Never pay anyone up front to search or to “release” a match. The search itself is always free.

Frequently asked questions

Is unclaimed-money recovery a scam?

No, the underlying system is real: every U.S. state runs a free, official unclaimed-property program, and MissingMoney.com and Unclaimed.org are the free, NAUPA-endorsed ways to search most of them. Scams exist around the edges, usually someone impersonating a government agency and asking for an upfront fee, but the databases themselves are legitimate.

Do I have to pay to search MissingMoney.com or Unclaimed.org?

No. Both are free to search and free to file a claim through. NAUPA's own guidance is explicit that there's no fee to search for and claim your unclaimed property if you use your official state program.

Is it ever legitimate to pay someone to find unclaimed money for me?

Yes. Paid finder or locator services are legal in most states. A legitimate one only gets paid a percentage of what they recover, and only after they recover it. Some states cap that percentage (California caps it at 10% under Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 1582); others don't regulate it as tightly. Check your own state's rule before signing anything.

How do I know if a call or text about unclaimed money is a scam?

The FTC’s warning signs: anyone asking for personal information or an upfront “processing” fee to release funds, or creating false urgency (“time is running out,” a claim period “extended just for you”). Real government unclaimed-property programs don’t call or text you first, and they don’t ask for payment to search or release your own money.

How long does the state hold my unclaimed money before it's gone for good?

In most states, there's no expiration. The state holds unclaimed property as custodian until claimed, and you or your heirs can claim it years or even decades later. This is different from some federal programs (like a specific tax year's unfiled-return refund), which do have hard deadlines; check the specific database for its own rule.

Why does the same money sometimes show up on both MissingMoney and my state's own site?

MissingMoney.com is a multi-state search covering most, but not every, state's program. Unclaimed.org is NAUPA's directory to each individual state's own site, which is the right fallback for any state MissingMoney doesn't fully cover.

Can I search for a deceased family member's unclaimed money?

Yes. Unclaimed property doesn't disappear when the owner dies. Heirs and estate representatives can search and file a claim, generally with proof of the relationship and the death.

The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge, Explained ($50/Month Wegovy & Zepbound)

The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge is a temporary CMS pilot offering certain GLP-1 weight-loss products for a flat $50 monthly copay. The eligibility rules are narrow, the covered-product list is exact, and the payment does not work like an ordinary Part D copay.

Last fact-checked: July 19, 2026

The short answer

The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge runs from July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027. If you satisfy every eligibility requirement, your copay is $50 per month regardless of income. The pilot covers only Foundayo tablets, Wegovy injections or tablets, and the Zepbound KwikPen. It does not cover Ozempic, compounded GLP-1s, or Zepbound single-dose pens or vials. Sources: CMS Medicare GLP-1 Bridge page; Medicare Fact Sheet, Product No. 12234

What is the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge?

The Bridge is a temporary CMS pilot operating under demonstration authority. It does not permanently rewrite Medicare drug law, and the existing Part D exclusion for weight-loss drugs remains unchanged.

The pilot began July 1, 2026 and is scheduled to end December 31, 2027. For people who meet every rule, it provides a flat $50 monthly copay for one of a limited number of covered products. Income does not change that amount. Sources: CMS; Medicare.gov: weight-loss drugs

The $50 does not count toward your Part D totals

Bridge payments do not count toward your Part D deductible or out-of-pocket maximum. The Extra Help program does not apply to the Bridge copay. Source: Medicare Fact Sheet, Product No. 12234

Exact Medicare GLP-1 Bridge eligibility rules

You must satisfy all of the following. Meeting only the age or BMI requirement is not enough.

  1. You must have eligible Part D coverage.
    Private Fee-for-Service (PFFS), cost plans and Programs of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE) do not qualify for this pilot.
  2. You must not already be receiving a GLP-1 through Part D.
  3. You must not have type 2 diabetes, moderate-to-severe sleep apnea, or fatty liver.
    According to the pilot rules, those conditions go through normal Part D coverage rather than the Bridge.
  4. You must be at least 18 years old.
  5. You must fit at least one of the following BMI and health-history groups:
    • BMI of 35 or higher; or
    • BMI of 30 or higher plus heart failure, uncontrolled hypertension, or chronic kidney disease stage 3a or higher; or
    • BMI of 27 or higher plus prediabetes, a prior heart attack, a prior stroke, or peripheral artery disease.

Sources: CMS Medicare GLP-1 Bridge page; Medicare Fact Sheet, Product No. 12234

Use the rules in order

First confirm your type of Part D coverage and whether you already receive a GLP-1 through Part D. Next check the excluded-condition rule. Only then compare your age, BMI and qualifying conditions with the three BMI pathways.

How to talk to your prescriber

Your prescriber decides what treatment is medically appropriate. Your job as a patient is to make sure the conversation uses the Bridge’s exact rules instead of relying on the general statement that “Medicare does not cover weight-loss drugs.”

You can bring this checklist to the appointment:

  • Confirm that you are age 18 or older.
  • Confirm your current BMI.
  • Review whether you have one of the listed conditions that goes with the BMI 30 or BMI 27 pathways.
  • Discuss whether you have type 2 diabetes, moderate-to-severe sleep apnea, or fatty liver, because the Bridge directs those conditions to normal Part D coverage.
  • Confirm whether you are already receiving a GLP-1 through Part D.
  • Show the prescriber the exact covered-product list, including the rule that only the Zepbound KwikPen is covered under the pilot.
  • Ask whether a covered Bridge product is medically appropriate for you.

A simple way to open the conversation

“CMS started the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge on July 1, 2026. I want to check whether I meet its exact Part D, BMI and health-history rules, and whether one of the covered products is appropriate for me.”

What if you do not qualify for the Bridge?

1. Check whether the Bridge is the wrong coverage route

If you have type 2 diabetes, moderate-to-severe sleep apnea, or fatty liver, the pilot says those conditions go through normal Part D, not the Bridge. That does not mean the Bridge should make an exception; it means you and your prescriber should discuss the ordinary Part D coverage route. Sources: CMS; Medicare.gov

2. Separate true ineligibility from an incorrect denial

An appeal can challenge a plan’s decision or an incorrect application of the rules. It does not change the Bridge’s eligibility rules. If, for example, you do not meet one of the BMI pathways, an appeal cannot rewrite the pilot’s threshold.

If you believe the decision is wrong, compare the denial with the exact criteria above. Check the drug formulation, your Part D arrangement, whether the decision treated a qualifying condition correctly, and whether the request was evaluated under the Bridge or normal Part D.

3. Consider self-pay pricing carefully

Manufacturer self-pay programs exist, but they are substantially more than the Bridge’s $50 monthly copay. Current verified prices are listed below. Prices and program terms can change, so check the manufacturer’s terms before paying.

4. Be cautious with compounded products

Compounded GLP-1s have not been broadly legal since the FDA shortages were resolved in December 2024 and February 2025. A narrow, patient-specific 503A exception remains. In April 2026, the FDA proposed tightening the rules further. Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration

If coverage is denied: the Part D appeal path

For a Part D drug request, the initial decision is called a coverage determination. The standard decision timeframe is 72 hours; an expedited determination is 24 hours.

  1. Coverage determination: the initial Part D decision.
  2. Redetermination: the first appeal, decided by the plan.
  3. Reconsideration: the next level, handled by an independent review entity.

Medicare beneficiaries can get free, one-to-one help from the State Health Insurance Assistance Program at SHIPhelp.org.

Appeals are often overlooked

KFF’s 2024 data found that 80.7% of appealed Medicare Advantage prior-authorization denials succeeded; that analysis was published in January 2026. KFF also found that 34% of appealed ACA Marketplace claim denials were overturned. Fewer than 1% of denials were appealed in the cited analyses. Source: KFF

Do not mix up Medicare and Marketplace deadlines

For ACA-compliant, non-grandfathered health plans, members generally have at least 180 days from the denial to file an internal appeal. After an internal denial, an independent external review may be available; the federal deadline is approximately four months, while state processes vary. These ACA rules are separate from the Part D coverage-determination, redetermination and reconsideration process.

Savings cards are not available to Medicare beneficiaries

Manufacturer savings cards are for eligible people with commercial insurance only. They cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid because of federal anti-kickback restrictions.

  • Wegovy savings card: as low as $25 per month, with a maximum $100 monthly benefit.
  • Zepbound savings card: as low as $25 per fill of up to three months; the card expires December 31, 2026.

Sources: NovoCare; Zepbound savings information

Frequently asked questions

Does Medicare cover Wegovy for $50 a month in 2026?

The Medicare GLP-1 Bridge provides covered Wegovy injections or tablets for a flat $50 monthly copay if you meet every pilot requirement. The Bridge runs from July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2027.

Does Medicare cover Zepbound through the GLP-1 Bridge?

Only the Zepbound KwikPen is covered by the Bridge. Zepbound single-dose pens and vials are not covered under the pilot.

Is Ozempic included in the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge?

No. Ozempic is not on the Bridge’s covered-product list.

Does income affect the $50 Bridge copay?

No. The Bridge copay is a flat $50 per month regardless of income. Extra Help does not apply to it.

Does the $50 payment count toward my Part D deductible or out-of-pocket maximum?

No. Bridge payments do not count toward the Part D deductible or out-of-pocket maximum.

Can I qualify based on BMI alone?

BMI alone can satisfy the health-threshold portion only if your BMI is at least 35. A BMI of at least 30 requires one of the listed accompanying conditions, while a BMI of at least 27 requires prediabetes or a listed cardiovascular history. You must still satisfy every other Bridge rule.

Can I use the Bridge if I have type 2 diabetes?

No. The Bridge excludes people with type 2 diabetes and directs those cases through normal Part D coverage. The same rule applies to moderate-to-severe sleep apnea and fatty liver.

Can I use a Wegovy or Zepbound savings card with Medicare?

No. The manufacturer savings cards are for eligible people with commercial insurance and cannot be used with Medicare or Medicaid.

Did the Bridge permanently end Medicare’s weight-loss drug exclusion?

No. The Bridge is a temporary pilot under demonstration authority. The Part D weight-loss drug exclusion remains unchanged.

Where can I get free help with a Medicare denial?

Contact your State Health Insurance Assistance Program through SHIPhelp.org. SHIP provides free Medicare assistance.

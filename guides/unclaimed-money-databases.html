9 Free Databases That May Hold Money in Your Name

State unclaimed property is only one bucket. Federal agencies separately hold unclaimed pensions, failed-bank and failed-credit-union deposits, VA insurance payouts, HUD refunds, and more, each in its own free database with its own rules about who it covers. All nine below were checked live for this guide; none of them require payment to search.

Last fact-checked: July 20, 2026

The short answer

Nine official, free databases can plausibly hold money in your name: two general state unclaimed-property portals (MissingMoney.com, Unclaimed.org), the IRS's unfiled-return refund process, and six agency-specific databases (PBGC pensions, FDIC failed-bank deposits, NCUA failed-credit-union deposits, VA life-insurance funds, HUD/FHA mortgage-insurance refunds, and NAIC's life-insurance locator for deceased policyholders). None of them charge a fee to search, and several have real restrictions worth knowing before you assume they apply to you. Those restrictions are covered honestly below, not glossed over.

1–2. MissingMoney.com and Unclaimed.org

MissingMoney.com is the official, free, multi-state unclaimed-property search run in partnership with participating state treasuries and endorsed by NAUPA (the National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators). It searches most states' databases at once, for free, updated daily. Source: MissingMoney.com

Unclaimed.org is NAUPA's own official directory, linking to every individual state's unclaimed-property program. It's the right fallback for any state MissingMoney doesn't fully cover, and a good place to start regardless. Source: Unclaimed.org

Why we list both

They overlap heavily but aren't identical: MissingMoney is faster for a multi-state sweep, while Unclaimed.org guarantees a path to every state's own site, including any state MissingMoney's aggregator doesn't fully cover.

3. IRS unfiled-return refunds

This works differently from the others: the IRS isn't holding a standing pool of “your money waiting.” Instead, if you never filed a return for a year you were owed a refund, you generally have three years from that return's original due date to file and claim it. After that, the money becomes the property of the U.S. Treasury. Source: IRS news release IR-2026-37

To see the scale: for tax year 2022, the IRS estimated $1.2 billion in refunds remained unclaimed for more than 1.3 million taxpayers who hadn't filed, with a median unclaimed refund of $686. That specific window closed April 15, 2026, but the same three-year mechanism opens again every year for the most recent filing year. Check the IRS's current guidance for this year's deadline. Source: IRS news release IR-2026-37

Separately, if a refund check the IRS already sent you was returned as undelivered mail, the “Where's My Refund?” tool at irs.gov/refunds can flag that and walk you through requesting a trace.

4. PBGC unclaimed pensions

The Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation's unclaimed-pensions database is free to search by last name and the last 4 digits of your Social Security number, and is updated quarterly. It covers PBGC-insured terminated defined-benefit plans, small-business defined-benefit plans, PBGC-insured multiemployer plans, and certain defined-contribution plans like 401(k)s under PBGC's Missing Participants Program. In other words: retirement money from an employer plan that ended and lost track of you. Source: PBGC: search unclaimed pensions

5. FDIC unclaimed funds

When the FDIC liquidates a failed bank, some deposits and checks go unclaimed. The FDIC's free search tool takes a name or check number (refinable by institution, city, or state). Deposits count as unclaimed if not asserted within 18 months of the bank's failure; after that, the FDIC transfers custody to the state of the depositor's last known address on file. Sources: FDIC unclaimed funds search; FDIC: unclaimed deposits information

6. NCUA unclaimed deposits

The credit-union equivalent of the FDIC list, with one important difference: it's not a name-search box. NCUA publishes a static, browsable table (last name, first and middle initial, city, state, credit union name) of unclaimed shares from liquidated federally-insured credit unions, and you scan it manually for a match. Still free, just a different format than you might expect. Source: NCUA: unclaimed deposits

7. VA unclaimed insurance funds

This free search covers death awards, dividend checks, and premium refunds owed under specific VA-administered life-insurance programs: U.S. Government Life Insurance, National Service Life Insurance, Veterans Special Life Insurance, Veterans Reopened Insurance, and Service-Disabled Veterans Life Insurance. Source: VA: unclaimed insurance funds search

It does NOT cover SGLI or VGLI

Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance and Veterans' Group Life Insurance (the policies most current and recent service members actually hold) are explicitly excluded from this tool. If that's your situation, contact the Office of Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance directly instead.

8. HUD/FHA mortgage-insurance premium refunds

If you had an FHA-insured mortgage, HUD sometimes owes a mortgage-insurance premium refund. There's a free search tool for it, but its own page states access is intended for people who already received HUD Form 27050-B (a refund-eligibility notice) directly from HUD within the past 180 days, or an agent with the owner's written consent. It is not an open search for anyone who's ever had an FHA loan. Source: HUD: FHA refund search

HUD's own warning

“You do not need to pay another person or firm to assist you in collecting your refund.” If you think HUD owes you a premium refund and haven't received Form 27050-B, HUD's own Mortgage Insurance Premium Refund Support Service Center is the direct contact, not a paid third party.

9. NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator

Run by the National Association of Insurance Commissioners, this free tool searches participating insurers for life-insurance policies and annuity contracts belonging to a deceased person. Per NAIC, it has connected consumers with more than $10 billion in unclaimed life-insurance and annuity benefits since its November 2016 launch. Source: NAIC: Life Insurance Policy Locator

It won't find your own forgotten policy

This tool exists specifically to help beneficiaries locate a deceased policyholder's coverage. It will not locate potential benefits for people who are alive. If you're trying to find a policy you personally took out and lost track of, contact the insurer directly instead.

What about savings bonds?

Older guides list TreasuryDirect's “Treasury Hunt” tool as a separate way to find unclaimed matured U.S. savings bonds. That tool was retired as of September 30, 2025. Inquiries about unclaimed Treasury securities are now handled through individual states' unclaimed-property programs instead, meaning Unclaimed.org (database #2 above) is now the right starting point, not a standalone federal bond-search tool. Source: TreasuryDirect: Treasury Hunt

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay to search any of these nine databases?

No. All nine are free, official government or NAUPA-endorsed tools. None of them require payment to search, and none require payment to file a claim once you find a match.

Which database should I check first?

Start with MissingMoney.com or Unclaimed.org, since state unclaimed property is the largest and most common category. From there, work down the list based on your situation. For example: PBGC if you had an old employer pension, or FDIC/NCUA if a bank or credit union you used ever failed.

Is the HUD refund search open to anyone who ever had an FHA loan?

No. HUD's own page restricts it to people who already received Form 27050-B, a refund-eligibility notice, directly from HUD within the past 180 days, or an authorized agent acting on that person's behalf with written consent.

Can the NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator find my own forgotten policy?

No. It's built specifically to help beneficiaries find a deceased person's life-insurance or annuity coverage. It will not locate a policy for someone who is still alive.

What happened to Treasury Hunt for savings bonds?

TreasuryDirect retired the Treasury Hunt tool as of September 30, 2025. Unclaimed Treasury securities inquiries now go through individual states' unclaimed-property programs, reachable through Unclaimed.org.

Is there really $70 billion in unclaimed money, like some sites claim?

NAUPA’s own site describes the total only as “billions” of dollars held by state governments, without publishing one specific, sourced total figure. We’re not repeating a precise number we couldn’t verify against a primary source. What is verifiable is that federal agencies alone have specific, dated figures, like the IRS’s estimated $1.2 billion in unclaimed refunds for tax year 2022.

Do I need my Social Security number to search these?

Some do, some don't. PBGC's pension search needs the last 4 digits of your SSN; MissingMoney and Unclaimed.org generally just need your name and state history; NCUA's list is browsed by name with no SSN involved at all. Check each tool's own instructions.

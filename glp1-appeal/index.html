Straight answers

What this kit can and cannot do.

“My doctor already tried.”

Your doctor may have submitted a prior authorization, but the denial should state why it failed. The worksheet helps the office compare that reason with the information the insurer requested. It does not replace your prescriber or tell the office to change your medical record.

“Appeals never work.”

Some appeals succeed and others do not. A documented plan exclusion may leave less room than a denial caused by missing records or unmet prior-authorization fields. The kit helps you identify that difference before you spend more time. It does not guarantee coverage, reimbursement, or a particular decision.

“I’ll just pay cash.”

Paying cash may be a workable choice, but recurring costs can add up and purchases outside your plan may not count toward your deductible or out-of-pocket limit. The Cost-Bridge Guide helps you compare legitimate routes while preserving any appeal rights. Confirm medication and treatment decisions with your licensed clinician.

“My employer plan dropped weight-loss coverage.”

A true benefit exclusion is different from a medical-necessity denial. The kit shows you how to request the controlling plan language, check whether the exclusion applies to your exact claim, and identify the correct complaint or employer-benefits route. It does not promise that an excluded benefit can be restored through an individual appeal.

“Is this a lawyer or appeal service?”

No. You receive consumer self-advocacy templates and process guides that you complete and submit yourself. The kit does not provide legal representation, medical advice, claim filing, or communication with your insurer.
